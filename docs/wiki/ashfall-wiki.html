Asheville has gone dark

Ashfall Field Manual

Ashfall is a zombie-survival modpack for Minecraft 26.3. A crashed vessel, the Wreck, brought a virus and strange energy technology to Asheville. You start in a safehouse on the edge of the dead city. Your goal is to scavenge, arm up, retake the city one district at a time and turn the power back on.

How to use this manual

The pages run in the order you'll need them: starter pages first, endgame last. Use the sidebar or the Previous and Next buttons at the bottom of each page. The search box looks through every page.

Your first ten minutes

  1. Get the Field Radio. Upstairs in the safehouse radio room. See Getting started.
  2. Learn what attracts zombies. Noise, light and blood. See Survival basics.
  3. Build a Spark Blaster. Scrap Metal, Scrap Electronics, Duct Tape and an Alloy Plate. See Key recipes.
  4. Follow the objective line. It sits above your hotbar and always shows your next step.

Getting started

You spawn in the front hall of the Montford safehouse at -152, -792. It is a safe zone: nothing spawns inside and zombies can't break in.

  1. Go upstairs to the radio room. Take the stairs in the north-west corner up one floor. The radio room is the north-east room. The cache chest sits against its south wall, under the desk corner. Everyone gets their own copy.
  2. Take the Field Radio and read the notes. The chest also holds the Survivor's Notes and 1–3 supplies. Right-click the radio for the grid report and to turn on the Grid HUD.
  3. Raid the pharmacy next door. It is two chunks east, at -120, -792, outside the safe zone. Look for bandages, batteries and sometimes antibiotics.
  4. Get clean water. Fill a bottle at any river or pond, then cook the Dirty Water in a furnace or campfire. Never drink it raw if you can help it.
  5. Be indoors and dark by nightfall. Darkness spawns more zombies, and they follow light and noise.

Survival basics

Zombies hunt with their senses. They react to noise, light and blood, and walk over to investigate whatever they notice. Staying quiet and dark keeps you alive.

Light

  • At night, standing in or holding light of level 7 or more lets zombies notice you from 4 blocks per light level. A torch in your hand is seen from about 56 blocks.
  • By day, only a bright room (level 10 or more, indoors) gives you away, and from less than a third as far.
  • A zombie must see the light. Light behind a solid wall does nothing.
  • Blackout curtains: wool, carpets, wool slabs and stairs, banners, closed doors and closed trapdoors all block a zombie's view. Glass does not. Hang wool over the windows of a lit room.
  • Light only matters near players. Lamps in an empty base attract nothing.

Light burns out

  • Torches you place last about 12 minutes, then go dark: a burnt-out torch is a charred stick with no light, so zombies see nothing either. Torches that came with the world never burn out, and nor do soul torches, copper torches or the one in your hand.
  • Relight a burnt-out torch with Matches (8 uses), Lighter Fluid (16 uses) or flint and steel: one use, another 12 minutes. Both salvage starters also light campfires, and Lighter Fluid still works as furnace fuel. Break a burnt-out torch for a stick.
  • Campfires last about 5 minutes, or 10 with extra fuel. Rain puts them out.
  • Electric lights last forever: lanterns and redstone lamps, crafted with Scrap Electronics. Use them in your base.

Blood

At half health or below you leave a blood trail that zombies can follow. Heal up before you head home.

Water and thirst

The droplets above your hunger bar are your thirst. It drains 1.5× faster than hunger, faster still in hot biomes. At 3 droplets or fewer you stop healing, and at 0 you can't sprint and start taking damage.

Where zombies come from

  • The dark. The darker a spot, the more zombies spawn there, out of sight. Dark interiors stay dangerous even by day.
  • Buildings. Each building has its own population, which spawns the first time someone comes near. Cleared buildings stay empty for 7 in-game days.
  • Never inside claims (the safehouse, your Bunker Core area, reclaimed compounds) or within 16 blocks of your bed.
  • Only zombies spawn as hostile mobs: no creepers, skeletons, spiders or phantoms. Animals are normal.

Zombies break and build

  • Zombies break blocks to reach you and pillar up with blocks of their own.
  • Fortified blocks slow them down: Reinforced Planks, Barricades, Reinforced Doors, iron blocks, iron bars, iron and copper doors and trapdoors, and obsidian. Zombies can't break these outright. Their hits wear them down until the block gives way to a weaker one, which they then break as usual (see Wear and repair). Bosses break fortified blocks outright. The Bunker Core never wears.
  • Broken blocks grow back after about 1.5 in-game days. Blocks zombies place vanish after 5 minutes.

  • Right-click to open it, from either hand. Hover over it to see what's inside.
  • Contents stay in the bag. If you die, it drops with everything still inside, so you can pick it all back up in one go.

Bicycles

  • Can't be crafted. Look for one leaning against a building wall on a city street. They're rare.
  • Ride it like a horse, no saddle needed: right-click to get on, steer with the mouse, W to pedal, S to back up slowly, sneak to get off.
  • Faster than sprinting and silent. It climbs kerbs and single steps but can't jump, and it's slow in water.
  • Sneak + right-click (or hit it) to pick it up. Right-click a block with it to put it down again.

Supply drops

  • Every 2 or 3 days, in the morning, chat announces a supply drop near one of 12 set points across the four districts. A green bar counts down 10 minutes.
  • The crate lands when you get within about 100 blocks: loudly, with 6 to 10 zombies around it. Smoke rises from it.
  • Inside: a charged Cell Pack or Energy Cell, food, water, bandages, parts, Arc Charges, and sometimes antibiotics, an attachment or a Grid Coupler.
  • Miss the deadline and it's lost. The Field Radio shows the distance, direction and time left.

Quiet weapons

Guns are loud, but melee makes no noise at all. The scrap melee weapons, the Nail Launcher, the Bottle Lure and the Arc Charge are on Weapons and ammo.

Sleeping

You can only sleep at home: beds (and backpack sleeping bags) work only inside your own or your party's Bunker Core claim, or a reclaimed substation compound. Anywhere else, the safehouse included, the bed refuses and doesn't set your respawn point; with no core and no reclaimed district you can't sleep at all. A host can turn this off with /gamerule ashfall:bunker_beds_only false. At home, beds work as normal at night when no zombie is near, and set your respawn point. Sleeping counts as a day survived, so new threats arrive sooner. You can't sleep on a horde night or while a district siege is set for tonight.

  • Sleeping costs water and food. You wake up 3 droplets thirstier and 2 shanks hungrier, as if you'd stayed up all night.
  • You can't sleep while you're thirsty or hungry: a bed refuses you with less than 3.5 droplets or 3.5 shanks left. Drink and eat before bed.
  • Lying down and getting up again (to set your respawn point) costs nothing. Only a night actually skipped does.

The mission: relight Asheville

The objective line above your hotbar always names your next step with its distance and direction. The full chain:

  1. Find the Field Radio in the safehouse radio room.
  2. Arm yourself with a Spark or Volt Blaster and a Cell Pack. See Key recipes.
  3. Optional: place a Bunker Core to claim a home base.
  4. Reclaim four districts, in any order. River Arts first is easiest; leave Mission for last.
  5. Collect 3 Resonance Crystals from the three story bosses, or from crash sites.
  6. Craft the Grid Uplink and run the Black Start finale at the Wreck hull.
  7. Vote Sever or Bind, then keep playing in the relit city.

Reclaiming a district

The short version is below. Full details, siege tables and rewards are on Reclaiming districts.

StepWhat to do
1. RepairRight-click the district's Substation Core with 4 Grid Couplers.
2. EnergizeLoad 4 fully charged Energy Cells, then hold the core for 5 minutes, staying within 32 blocks. It hums loudly and draws zombies, so fortify the doors first.
3. Link relaysRight-click the district's 6 key Power Relays with a Grid Coupler. It isn't used up. The radio points to the nearest one.
4. SiegeAt the next nightfall, waves attack the core. Clear them all, or keep players near the core until dawn.

Losing a siege costs time, not the district. You keep the couplers and relay links, but must load 4 fresh cells and redo the hold.

The finale

With all four districts reclaimed the Wreck hull opens. The Grid Uplink takes 3 Resonance Crystals, 4 Focusing Lenses, 8 Grid Couplers and 16 Energy Cells in total. Bring at least 16 more charged cells for the fight itself.

Building types

Big buildings that span several chunks always have the same type: a factory is a workshop, a town hall is a government building, and so on. Ordinary one-chunk buildings take their type from the district they stand in, so the same block of flats is a home in Montford, an office Downtown and a warehouse in River Arts. These photos are from one world; yours will be arranged differently.

Building keys and Strongboxes

Some banks, police stations, offices, hotels, pharmacies and other high-value buildings (about 2 in 5) hide a locked Strongbox on one floor. It holds better loot than the shelves around it, more so in harder districts: supplies, valuables, attachments, Cell Packs, and in the toughest districts a small chance of a rare blaster.

  • The key: one zombie in that building carries a Building Key and drops it when it dies. The key's tooltip says which building it opens ("Key: Bank at X, Z"). Right-click the box with the right key to open it quietly; the key is used up.
  • The Crowbar: no key? Hold right-click on the box with a Crowbar for 4 seconds to force it. It's loud (zombies hear it 48 blocks away) and costs 31 durability, so a new Crowbar forces about 8 boxes. The Crowbar is also a decent melee weapon (4.5 damage).
  • A locked Strongbox can't be broken, blown up, emptied by a hopper or broken by zombies. Once open it stays open, and you can mine it with a pickaxe and take it home as an 18-slot chest.
  • Each building gets one box, ever: it doesn't come back when the building refills.

Weapons and ammo

Every weapon is shown the same way, on the same scales, so you can compare any two. Damage is per hit (the Lancer's 28 fills the bar). Per minute is shots or swings (600 a minute fills it). Heard at is how far away zombies hear it, in blocks (64 fills it): amber is quiet enough for sneaking, red carries far. For scale, an iron sword does 6 damage at 96 swings a minute.

Recipes are on All recipes. "Rare loot" means crash sites, bosses and the Mission reward.

Polished Arms (optional look): the pack includes a resource pack, Ashfall Polished Arms, that repaints every blaster, both Plasma Blades, the Flamethrower and the Arc Charge as dark machined gunmetal at twice the texture detail, with knurled rubber grips and no rust or tape. Shapes, glow colours and stats are unchanged. Turn it on in Options → Resource Packs; it is off by default.

Bases and co-op

The safehouse

Anyone can open its doors, chests and barrels, use its furnaces and beds, read books, and use the Field Radio, medical items, food and blasters. Nobody can break or place blocks there.

Reclaimed compounds

A reclaimed substation's compound is a safe zone that works like a Bunker Core claim for every player: build, break, place and use items, sleep in beds and set up Rain Collectors. Nothing spawns there and zombies break nothing. It stops being one while a Breach is on, or if the district goes dark.

Your Bunker Core

  • Placing it claims an 80×80-block area for you or your party. Nothing spawns inside, and Field Chargers work.
  • The claim doesn't shield survivors or pets: zombies that get inside can hurt them and infect survivors, so wall them in on horde nights.
  • Horde waves can still break plain blocks inside the claim. Build with fortified blocks: Reinforced Planks, barricades, Reinforced Doors, iron blocks, iron bars, and iron or copper doors and trapdoors. They wear down under attack, so repair them after a horde night (see Wear and repair).
  • Sealing yourself in doesn't stop a horde night. A wave that finds no way in stands at your wall for a few seconds, then breaks through toward the core, even if it never sees anyone. Once a zombie sees you or a survivor, it goes for them instead. Zombies can't place blocks inside the claim, so they come through your walls, not over them.
  • You respawn at your core first, then your bed, then the nearest safe zone. After respawning you get 10 seconds of protection, which ends when you attack.
  • Right-click the core with an empty hand to open the transit network (below).

Stray Dogs roam the residential zones (Montford and the suburbs). Tame one with Rotten Flesh or a bone: each piece has a 1 in 3 chance. Strays are rare: each part of the map only ever gets one, so a stray that dies or is tamed is not replaced. Ordinary wolves can be tamed the same way.

Survivors

Not everyone in Asheville has turned. Survivors are people you can rescue, bring home and put to work. Each has a name, their own look, and a nameplate showing their job: "Rae (Guard)".

Finding them

  • Distress calls: from day 7, when you finish a distress call the caller is standing by the reward chest. They follow the nearest player within 24 blocks whose group still has room, so a full player standing closest doesn't cost a friend the rescue.
  • Holdouts: from day 7, a house, hotel, office block, school or shop sometimes has someone hiding on an upper floor (a 6% chance the first time anyone comes near the building, so only buildings nobody visited before day 7; never landmarks or claimed land). Zombies can't find a holdout while they keep quiet. When you come within 12 blocks they call out to you, once, and from then on the building's zombies can hear them too, so clear the floors before you go up. Right-click them and click [Follow]: the first player to give an order takes them in. If your group already has 6 survivors, or the world is at its limit, they stay where they are.
  • Survivors never despawn. If they die, they're gone for good.

Changing jobs

  • New job, first day slow: a survivor works at half speed for one in-game day after you change their job. Talk to them and they'll say they're still learning. The job a survivor came with has no learning day.
  • Not on horde nights: on a horde night, from the moment the siren warns until dawn, survivors refuse to change jobs: "Not now. They're coming." Follow, Stay and Home still work.
  • A scavenger who is out can't be given a new job until they're back.

Food

  • Every survivor you lead eats once a day, the least filling food first, until they've had enough. Living at home (settled and sent [Home]) they eat from the home's Supply Lockers.
  • Away from home (following you, or told to [Stay] out in the city, home or not) they eat from your own inventory instead, as long as you're online and within 32 blocks. They never touch the home lockers while they're away. Keep some food on you on long trips: talk tells you when they're hungry.
  • A day's meal is 5 hunger points, the same as one loaf of bread. A cooked steak (8) covers a day by itself; berries or melon slices (2 each) take 3. A core's first 3 settlers need 5 points each; from the 4th settler on, each needs 10 (away from home it's always 5).
  • Survivors refuse rotten flesh, spider eyes and poisonous potatoes, however hungry they are. Keep the rotten flesh for taming dogs. They also never take Bottled Water, golden apples or enchanted golden apples, so those are safe in a locker or your pockets.
  • If there isn't enough for their whole meal, they eat nothing and go hungry: their nameplate says "hungry" and they work at half speed. Hungry and new to a job, they work at a quarter speed.
  • After 3 hungry days in a row a survivor leaves for good, and their leader is told. Leaving from home, they put their weapon and anything they carry in the lockers first; leaving from anywhere else, they drop them where they stand.
  • Holdouts still hiding and traders never eat. Days a survivor spends unloaded (nobody near) aren't counted: they eat once when they load again.

Supply Locker

A steel locker with 27 slots, like a chest. It can only be placed inside a Bunker Core's area. Make it from 7 Scrap Metal, a chest and an iron ingot (All recipes). When a guard stops being a guard, their weapon goes into the nearest Supply Locker at their home. If there's no room, they drop it at their feet. The base jobs and the daily meals take what they need from these lockers, and scavengers put their finds in them. Only lockers in loaded chunks of the core's area count. Carry On can't pick up a Supply Locker, so it stays in the bunker.

Danger

  • Zombies hunt survivors the way they hunt you: a zombie goes for whoever is nearest, you or a survivor, so survivors following you can be bitten while you fight. Once it has picked someone it only switches if another target is clearly closer. Special infected only hunt players.
  • Bites: a zombie's hit can infect a survivor, with the same chance as for you. Their nameplate then says "bitten", and their leader is told. One in-game day later they turn into a zombie, still holding their weapon. A bitten survivor who dies of anything else also turns.
  • Cure: right-click a bitten survivor with Antibiotics. One dose cures them completely.
  • No friendly fire: nothing a player does can hurt a survivor: weapons, arrows, pets, explosions and Spike Strips all spare them. Survivors can't hurt each other either. They also shrug off lava, fire, suffocation and falling anvils or blocks, so nobody can use those on them. Only zombies (and other hostile mobs) can kill a survivor.

Limits

  • Each player and their party can have up to 6 survivors, settled or following. A Bunker Core holds 6 settlers. The whole world allows 30 taken-in survivors; holdouts still hiding, unfound, don't count toward that.
  • When everyone near a finished distress call is at their group's limit, or the world is at its limit, the call leaves only the note, and chat says which limit it was.
  • While the world is at its limit, no new holdouts appear, and a holdout won't join anyone until there's room.

Step 1: Repair

Right-click the core with 4 Grid Couplers, one at a time. Each is used up. The core becomes Repaired.

Step 2: Link 3 key relays

  • Each district has 6 key Power Relays around it. Link any 3 by right-clicking them with a Grid Coupler. It isn't used up. Pick the ones nearest the core.
  • Linking works as soon as the core is repaired. Do it by day, before loading any cells, so the noisy hold and the siege come on the same night.
  • You can still link during the hold, so in co-op one player can run relays while others hold the core.
  • The Field Radio names the nearest relay you haven't tuned into, and the Grid Map shows the ones you have.

Step 3: Energize

  • Right-click the core with 4 fully charged Energy Cells (240 each).
  • The last cell starts a 5-minute hold, shown on a boss bar. It only counts while a player is within 32 blocks.
  • The core hums every 4 seconds, loud enough to pull zombies from 144 blocks. Extra zombies keep arriving: 2 every 15 s, up to 8 at once.
  • Fortify the control-room doors before you load the last cell.

What reclaiming gives

  • +20% Grid for the whole world, up to 80% with all four.
  • A safe compound: the core's chunk and the chunks around it become a safe zone that works like a Bunker Core claim for everyone: build, break, sleep and set up Rain Collectors there.
  • A transit stop: fast travel to and from the substation (see Bases).
  • 80% fewer zombies spawning from the dark across the district.
  • Free charging: Field Chargers and Power Relays in the district need no cells.
  • A reward cache at the core, one per player, plus the district's extra from the table above.
  • A trader at the substation, with the district's own stock (see Traders).
  • The lights come back: dead lamps become Grid Lamps as you explore. Lit streets draw zombies at night.
  • Every later siege and horde night gets a tier harder.

Keeping the power on

A reclaimed district has to be kept up. Its linked relays wear down, and a district needs 3 working relays to stay lit.

  • Wear: each linked relay loses 8% a day, starting 3 days after the district is reclaimed. Left alone, a relay lasts about 12 days.
  • Horde nights: after each horde night, one reclaimed district takes the hit: 1–2 of its relays lose 30%.
  • Flicker: with fewer than 3 working relays, the district flickers. Everyone is told, and you have 2 days to fix it.
  • Blackout: after that the district goes dark. It loses its safe compound, free power, Grid % and spawn reduction until fixed. No new siege: repair enough relays and it comes straight back.
  • Repair: use a Wire Spool on a linked relay with Duct Tape in your inventory. Both are used up, and the relay is back to 100%.
  • Check a relay: use a Grid Coupler on it to see its condition and how many relays work.
  • Spares: once a district is reclaimed, you can link more of its 6 key relays with a Grid Coupler. Each spare is one more relay that has to fail before the lights do.
  • The Field Radio lists worn relays (30% or less), flickering districts with days left, and blackouts.

What happens when relays fail

One relay over time

Admins tune this in data/ashfall/ashfall/grid/decay.json: about 4 wear per day is relaxed, 15 means daily upkeep, and "enabled": false turns decay off. /ashfall grid relays <district> <0-100> sets a district's relays for testing.

Breaches

  • Every 7 days from day 7, a Breach hits a random reclaimed district. The siren names it 150 s before nightfall. A Breach never lands on a horde night, the night after one, or a District Siege night: it waits for the next free night.
  • Free Defend trip: each siren comes with a [Defend] button in chat (or /ashfall travel defend). It takes you to the breached substation from anywhere, free, after the usual 3-second hold-still, even on a horde night or while a zombie is hunting you. One trip per player per Breach.
  • It's a siege on that core. While it runs, the district loses its safe compound and free power.
  • You have to defend it. The Breach is held if the core is still standing at dawn and players spent at least 4 minutes within 96 blocks of it, or if you clear every wave. The boss bar counts the defence time down. Stay away and the district is lost at dawn.
  • Every held Breach leaves a supply cache at the core (each player gets their own roll): an Energy Cell, salvage and parts, sometimes a charged Standard pack or a Resonance Crystal.
  • If it falls, or nobody defends it, the district goes Broken: dark again, no free power, no safe compound. Socket 2 Grid Couplers to restore it at once. You keep your links, reward and Grid %.

Relay Logs

Hold the Field Radio and use any Power Relay to tune in. You get a Relay Log book, one of 30 fragments telling what happened to Asheville. Each player collects their own, and they survive death. Three more documents are in the Kiln & Co. office, the hospital supply room and the Wreck hull. Your logs fill your epilogue at the end.

The Uplink

  • While it runs it's the loudest and brightest thing in the game: a 192-block sound and a 128-block light every 4 seconds.
  • It has 1200 HP. Every zombie within 6 blocks takes 1 HP per second, including zombies battering the outside of the hull wall next to it, so don't only guard the hatch.
  • Right-click it with a Grid Coupler to patch 200 HP.

Phase 1: Surge

  • Goal: 450 seconds of powered time. The Uplink brings 60 s; each full Energy Cell adds 45 s, up to 600 s banked. If the buffer runs dry, progress stops.
  • Waves every 30 s. When it ends, River Arts and South Slope light up below the ridge (Grid 87%).

Phase 2: Feedback

  • Goal: close both Relay Breakers, which rise 20 blocks either side of the socket. Each needs a living player within 3 blocks for 75 s. Both can run at once.
  • An unattended breaker keeps its progress for a short grace period (20 s solo, less in groups), then slips back.
  • Special infected pour out of two vents every 20 s, and a feedback arc hits the Uplink for 60 HP every 30 s.
  • Waves every 45 s. When it ends, Downtown lights up (Grid 94%).

Phase 3: The Heart

  • Goal: break the four Heart pylons. A lit Plasma Blade takes about 10 s each; a pickaxe about 2 minutes.
  • The Choir rises beside the Heart: a crystal giant with 420 HP, 12 damage and armour 10, immune to knockback. Kill it or keep it busy while someone works the pylons. It shatters when the last pylon falls.
  • Waves every 60 s. When it ends, Mission lights up, the Grid reaches 100%, and everyone who took part gets Asheville Relit.

Scaling and failing

  • Waves scale with the players near the socket: 13 zombies a wave solo, 18 for two, 22 for three, 25 for four.
  • Failing costs time, not the run. The run pauses if the Uplink reaches 0 HP or nobody stays within 96 blocks for 20 s. You lose 60 s of Surge, or both breakers trip open again. Won phases and broken pylons stay done.
  • To resume, feed a cell or patch with a coupler once the Uplink is back to at least 50% HP. The boss bar says what's missing.
